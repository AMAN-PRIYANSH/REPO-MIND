## Limitations

- D3c has a handful of applications chosen by written rules from a fixed candidate list; rates describe these cases, not Go software in general.
- Cases of one application share code, toolchain and dependencies, so they are not independent; Wilson intervals assume independence and therefore understate the uncertainty.
- Truth is adjudicated by reviewer agents and the supervisor reading the source, with an owner spot-check (the CP7b method). It is not expert human ground truth; reviewers of the same model family share blind spots.
- Truth asks whether any decision target is reachable by some route: the curated vulndb symbols in stratum S, the fix's changed non-context functions in stratum N. Reachability only, never exploitability.
- Stratum N includes UNREVIEWED (GHSA-imported) advisories, whose affected ranges and fix links the Go security team has not checked; both tools read the same pinned snapshot.
- D3 and D3b are IN-SAMPLE for CP10 (the hygiene rules were written after reading their adjudications); their earlier truths (CP7b, CP9) were judged on the legacy targets (the changed functions), while the CP10 truth-only items are judged on the hygiene targets.
- Analysis is for linux/amd64, CGO_ENABLED=0, no build tags, go1.26.10; other configurations can differ.
